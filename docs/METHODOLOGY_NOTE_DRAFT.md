# DRAFT — for review, not published

Proposed addition to `/methodology`. Nothing is live. It ships **with** the norms pause, never
instead of it.

Length: one section, roughly 350 words. Placed after the evidence-tier table and before the
refusals, so a reader meets it as part of how the instruments work rather than as an apology.

---

## What our numbers rest on

Every instrument here reports a number. This is where each of those numbers comes from, including
the two places where the honest answer is "not from as much as you'd want".

**The Personality Mirror and the EI Mirror are established instruments.** Five-factor structure
and a four-domain emotional intelligence model are among the most replicated findings in the
field. That is a statement about the constructs, not about our arithmetic, and the two are worth
keeping apart.

**Our own instruments are developmental and labelled as such.** The Essential, Closeness and
Everyday Mirrors use questions we wrote. Their properties are still being established. The
Everyday Mirror has not yet been tested for whether one option in each pair sounds more
flattering than the other, which is a real gap and stated on the instrument itself.

**We have stopped comparing you with other people.** Until recently, a personality factor was
reported as how many people sit further toward one end than you, and each factor carried a band
word — very high, average, very low. Both of those are claims about a population. When we went
looking for the population, we could not establish one: the score bands we inherited record no
sample size, no group and no date, all fifteen factors share the same generic lower cut-offs, and
the band widths look like an even split of the possible range rather than a map of how people
actually score.

So we removed those statements rather than printing a caveat beside them. A footnote admitting
the comparison rests on nothing, under a sentence making the comparison anyway, is worse than
either on its own.

What is left is everything that never needed a population: which way you lean on each factor, and
which three sit furthest from your own middle. Those are facts about your answers.

**What that still rests on.** Naming your three furthest requires knowing how much of the
distance is signal and how much is measurement noise, and we have not measured that for these
item banks either. The threshold we use — a factor has to sit a stated distance from your own
average before we name it — is a deliberately conservative provisional choice, not a figure
derived from our own data. When we have measured it, that number will change, and we will say so
here first.

If a reference sample is ever built, we will publish how many people are in it and who they are
**before** any comparison appears in a reading.

## Notes for you, not for the page

1. **The last two paragraphs are the ones I'd expect pushback on internally** and the ones I'd
   fight to keep. Every competitor publishes percentiles without a word about where the norms came
   from. Saying "we looked for ours and couldn't establish it" is the whole brand in three
   sentences.
2. **I have not named the parent product** in the paragraph about inherited bands. It's accurate
   either way; naming it makes this a statement about My Mirror Report's norms, which is a
   different decision and yours to take.
3. **No number appears in the draft** — not the 1.5-sten floor, not the α values. Once one goes
   in, a reader reasonably asks for the rest, and the rest is the internal spec.
4. **This does not mention the MRD gates**, deliberately. They run in shadow and change nothing a
   reader sees; describing machinery that does not affect the output invites the question of why
   it exists.
5. If you want it shorter, the EI/Personality paragraph is the one to cut — it repeats the
   evidence-tier table immediately above it.
